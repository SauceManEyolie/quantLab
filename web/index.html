What your strategy says right now

Every term, in plain English

The unlocking idea: every indicator here is just a function over a rolling window of numbers. There's no hidden theory. RSI is about eight lines of pandas — you can read all of them in indicators.py in ten minutes. The finance world gives these functions intimidating names; the maths is not intimidating.

Terms are also underlined like this throughout the app — hover any of them for the short version without leaving what you're doing.

How to read this thing

Plain English, no finance degree required.

The one number that decides everything

vs Buy & Hold. Bitcoin went up a lot over most historical windows. A strategy that returns 40% sounds great until you notice buy-and-hold returned 90% in the same period — you did more work, paid more fees, took more risk, and made less. Beating buy-and-hold is the entire game. Most strategies don't. That's not a bug in the app; that's the actual result, and knowing it before you risk money is worth more than any signal.

Max drawdown > total return

Max drawdown is the worst peak-to-trough fall your account took. A strategy that returns 200% with a 70% drawdown is one you will abandon at the bottom — everyone does. Drawdown is the number that predicts whether you'll actually still be running the strategy in six months.

Win rate is the most misleading stat here

A 90% win rate means nothing if the 10% of losses are each 20× the size of a win. Look at profit factor (gross wins ÷ gross losses — above 1.5 is decent, above 2 is strong) and expectancy (average dollars per trade). Trend strategies often win 35% of the time and make money anyway.

Sharpe ratio

Return per unit of bumpiness. Below 1 is weak, 1–2 is respectable, above 2 in a backtest usually means you've overfit something. Be suspicious of your own good results.

Why the numbers here are lower than other tools

  • Signals fire on a bar's close and fill at the next bar's open. You never get the price that made you want to trade.
  • Fees are charged on both sides of every round trip.
  • Slippage assumes you buy a little high and sell a little low.
  • When a bar could have hit both your stop and your target, we assume the stop hit first.
  • The still-forming candle is dropped, so no partial-future data leaks in.

Every one of those is a place where sloppier backtesters quietly manufacture profit. The pessimism is the feature.

The overfitting trap

Test enough parameter combinations and some will look spectacular by pure chance. That's what the Optimize tab's in-sample/out-of-sample split is for. If parameters that shine on the first 70% of history fall apart on the last 30%, you found a coincidence, not an edge. This is the single most common way people lose money with backtesting.

What this tool cannot tell you

It uses closed candles, so it can't model intrabar order fills, order-book depth, funding rates, exchange outages, or your own behaviour under stress. Backtests are a filter for bad ideas, not proof of good ones. A strategy that fails here will almost certainly fail live. One that succeeds here has merely earned the right to be paper-traded next.

Nothing in this application is financial advice, and past performance genuinely does not predict future returns. Crypto is volatile enough to lose the entire position. Treat every number here as a research output, not a recommendation.
